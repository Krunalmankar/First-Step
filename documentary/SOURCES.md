# Research sources: "Could the United States Collapse Because of Its Debt?"

Figures in the script were verified as of **11 October 2026** (per the brief). Fiscal year 2026 ran 1 Oct 2025 – 30 Sep 2026.

> **Production caveat.** This build ran in a sandbox with no network access to Treasury, CBO, FRED, IMF or
> agency websites, so I could not re-check the figures against their primary sources. On-screen numbers use
> the figures the brief supplied. Where a chart needed extra data points, I took values from the published
> series as I know them; those points are marked *approximate* on screen and listed under "Values to
> re-verify" below. Check them before you publish.

## Description-ready source list

- US Treasury, *Debt to the Penny* (total public debt outstanding $40.31 trillion, 8 October 2026): https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/debt-to-the-penny/
- CBO, *Monthly Budget Review: September 2026* (FY2026 deficit, outlays, revenue, net interest, defense, Social Security, Medicare), 8 October 2026: https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61985
- CBO, *The Budget and Economic Outlook: 2026 to 2036*, February 2026: https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61882
- IMF, COFER data brief for Q2 2026, 30 September 2026: https://data.imf.org
- 30-year Treasury yield (constant maturity), FRED series DGS30: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS30; Treasury auction results: https://www.treasurydirect.gov
- Debt limit ($41.104 trillion, set 4 July 2025) and the 2027 timing estimate: National Taxpayers Union Foundation, *Projecting the Debt Limit*: https://www.ntu.org/publications/detail/projecting-the-debt-limit
- UK 2022 gilt crisis: Bank of England, letter from Deputy Governor Jon Cunliffe to the Treasury Committee, October 2022: https://www.bankofengland.co.uk
- Credit rating actions: S&P Global Ratings (August 2011), Fitch Ratings (August 2023), Moody's Ratings (May 2025) press releases

## Claim to confirm before publishing

- *"So far, every time America has put its bonds up for sale, the world has shown up to buy them."* To my knowledge no US Treasury auction has failed (some have "tailed", meaning they cleared at a higher yield than expected, but every one has been fully covered). I could not check this against TreasuryDirect auction records from the sandbox. If you can't confirm it, change the line to "So far, the world has kept buying." The script line already starts with "So far", which keeps the claim modest.

## Values to re-verify (supporting chart points, marked "approximate" on screen)

These shape the context lines in three charts. They are **not** narrated figures.

- **30-year yield, annual averages 2007–2025** (`data.py: YIELD_30Y`): check against FRED DGS30, annual frequency, average aggregation. The 2025 value matters most.
- **USD share of allocated reserves, 2000–2024** (`data.py: USD_SHARE`): check against the IMF COFER year-end values.
- **Debt held by the public as % of GDP, 1940–2024** (`data.py: DEBT_GDP_HIST`): check against CBO's historical budget data. The projection line uses only the CBO anchor points the script cites (101% in 2026, the record crossed in 2030, 120% in 2036), joined by straight dashed segments. On screen it is labelled "simplified".
- **Rome silver-content bars** (`data.py: ROME_SILVER`): about 95%, 50% and 5%. Check against Harl (1996) or
  Walker (1976–78); published estimates vary by a few points.
- **UK gilt-yield line** is a schematic of the shape only. It is labelled "schematic shape, not to scale" and has no axis values.

If any value changes, edit `data.py` and run `python3 build.py --skip-tts` again. The charts pick up the new numbers automatically.
